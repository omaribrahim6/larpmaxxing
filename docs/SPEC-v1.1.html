Roblox game spec · v1.1 · 11 Sep 2026

Larpmaxxing

[LARP Simulator]

Run around grabbing performative props, rank up from NPC to LARP Maxxer, and larp-off anyone you meet. Every round is a short, ridiculous skit, and upsets can happen.

What changed in v1.1

  • The public title is now Larpmaxxing [LARP Simulator]. "Larpmaxxing" stays the brand.
  • Scenes are now the top development priority. Each stat has a full scene sheet: six tiers, a takeover by the winner, and fumble variants for the loser. Rounds are longer, so a larp-off now runs about 30 seconds instead of 23.
  • New sections: Stamps & moments for how every stamp looks and sounds, and Built for vertical for framing rules and the new Clip Mode.

The pitch

The scenes are the product. The simulator loop gives players a reason to reach them again and again.

"Larping" means pretending to be something you're not, and in 2026 being "performative" is the most visible form of it: matcha in hand, tote bag on the shoulder, a book nobody reads. Larpmaxxing turns that into a simulator. You collect props, your stats go up, your rank title goes up, and you prove it by out-larping other players in larp-offs. Each round of a larp-off is a skit that works as a TikTok or Shorts clip on its own.

  • Stupid-simple. A new player understands everything within a minute: grab things, get promoted, larp-off people.
  • Every round is a skit. A larp-off is about 30 seconds of escalating scenes. The winner takes over the set, the loser fumbles, and the crowd reacts. That's what gets posted.
  • Welcoming for new players. Early ranks come fast, losing costs nothing, and upsets mean even a near-even fight can go either way.
  • For everyone. Every rank, item and title is gender-neutral. It's about props, not looks.

Core loop

Collect props→ Stats go up→ Rank up→ Larp-off: five skits→ Win bonus points ↺ back to collecting

Items appear across the map. Walking into one collects it, and its points go straight into one of five stats. There's no inventory to manage. Your total across all five stats sets your rank. Higher stats unlock bigger, sillier versions of each scene, which is the real reward for farming. At any point you can challenge a nearby player to a larp-off.

Larp-offs

Challenging

  1. Walk within 12 studs of a player and press the Larp-off prompt.
  2. They see "omar_larps wants to larp-off" with Accept and Decline, plus a 10-second timer that auto-declines.
  3. On accept, both players teleport to a free stage in the Plaza. If all four are busy, they queue and the HUD shows their place in line.
  4. After the verdict, both return to where they were standing.
  • After a decline, the challenger can't challenge that same player again for 30 seconds.
  • A settings toggle, Accept larp-offs, lets people farm in peace. It's on by default.
  • Each stage has spectator stands. Anyone nearby watches live, and the crowd's reactions are part of the scene (see Stamps & moments).

The sequence

All five rounds always play, even after someone has clinched it. A 5–0 has more bragging power than a 3–0. Rounds are 4.5 seconds, long enough for a complete skit with a setup, an escalation and a payoff.

Resolving a match

  • Each round compares one stat after the upset roll. The higher rolled value wins the round.
  • If both players have 0 in a stat, that round is a draw.
  • Most rounds won takes the match. If it's level, for example 2–2 with a draw, the higher combined rolled total wins.

Stakes

  • Winner: +1 Win, plus bonus points split evenly across the stats they won rounds in.
  • Loser: loses nothing. They get the red EXPOSED tag on their nameplate for 3 minutes and a Rematch button.
  • Upset winner: the bonus is doubled and the stage plays the full UPSET! moment.
-- winner's bonus points
base       = max(25, 2% of the winner's total points)
rankFactor = 1 + 0.2 × (opponent rank − winner rank), clamped to 0.6 … 1.8
bonus      = base × rankFactor × (2 if upset else 1)

The rank factor is deliberately gentle: beating someone three ranks above you pays 1.6×, and beating someone three ranks below pays 0.6×. There's a reason to punch up, but low-ranked players are never worthless opponents and the scale never feels impossible.

Win trading: full rewards for the first two larp-offs against the same player in any 10-minute window, then zero. Larp-offs against them still play, for fun, but they pay nothing.

Upsets

Every round, each player's stat is rolled before the comparison. Upsets are a headline feature, so the variance is tuned high enough that close fights are genuinely uncertain, but big gaps still hold. This system is unchanged from v1.0.

-- per player, per round (server-side only)
rolled = stat × random(0.70, 1.30)          -- ±30% "bad selfie day"
if random() < 0.10 then rolled ×= 2.0 end   -- 10% VIRAL MOMENT

An upset needs you to be close. A Normie can't beat a LARP Maxxer, but two players near the same level never know how it'll go.

  • Specialists still win. A player with three maxed stats beats an evenly spread player with the same total 67% of the time. Choosing a build matters, and the dice don't erase it.
  • Rolls drive the scenes. Scene tiers come from the rolled value, not the base stat, so what spectators see always matches who wins. A Viral Moment visibly launches a player up one or more tiers mid-round, which is the best comeback footage the game can produce.

Scenes

This is the product. Everything else exists to get players here.

A round must never feel like a stat comparison with props on top. It's a 4.5-second skit that makes sense to someone watching with no context, is funny with the sound off, and ends with a clear winner before any number appears. Scene polish is the top development priority, ahead of item count, zones and monetization.

Rules for every scene

  • The climb. Both players start each round at Tier 1 and escalate together, one tier at a time. When a player reaches their tier, they stop climbing and hold their pose while the other keeps going. Spectators can see who's winning as it happens.
  • Every tier changes the world, not just the prop. Each step up adds environment, animation, camera and crowd changes. By Tier 5 the set looks nothing like Tier 1.
  • The winner takes over. The round winner's scene spills across onto the loser's set: their car parks there, their spotlight swings over, their crowd turns around.
  • The loser fumbles, readably. Each stat has several fumble variants chosen at random, so repeat larp-offs don't produce identical clips. Fumbles are cartoonish and never show injury.
  • Same tier, face-off. If both players stop at the same tier, the camera cuts between them twice, then the higher roll takes over.

Production notes

  • Custom animations are required for launch. Built-in emotes are only for blocking out the MVP. Every tier, takeover and fumble gets its own animation made in Studio's Animation Editor.
  • Scenes are data. Each tier, takeover and fumble is an entry in the scene config pointing to a prop set, an animation, camera keys, sounds and crowd reactions. New fumbles and tiers can ship weekly without new code.
  • Captions for key sounds. Words like SNORE, CRACK and BEEP BEEP BEEP pop up as text, so the skit still works when a clip is watched muted.

Camera & crowd toolkit

  • Shots: low-angle hero, whip pan, punch-in zoom, slow-motion, freeze frame, pull-out reveal (for Maxxed), and slow orbit (for the verdict).
  • Screen shake and flashes are used only at Tier 4 and above and on the big moments. The Reduce effects setting turns both off.
  • The crowd is spectator NPCs in the stands plus any real players watching. Reactions get bigger the closer the round was and the higher the tiers reached.

Built for vertical

Clips of larp-offs will be watched on phones, cropped to 9:16. A full-height 9:16 crop of a 16:9 screen keeps only the middle 31.6% of its width, so anything that happens outside that strip won't be in the clip.

Revised September 11, 2026, after the vertical-slice playtest. Squeezing two players, two vehicles and a takeover into the middle 31.6% ruins the widescreen scene for everyone playing. So the two jobs are split: the normal larp-off is shot for the screen it's played on, and Clip Mode re-shoots it for phones.

  • Default: full widescreen. Both-player shots, vehicle arrivals and takeovers use the whole 16:9 frame. Nothing is pinned to the centre strip.
  • Stamps land dead-centre and at a size that's readable on a phone. That costs the widescreen shot nothing, and a crop keeps them.
  • Hide the HUD during key beats. Only the round dots and stat name stay, in the top-centre.
  • Clip Mode switches the camera; it doesn't crop. With the setting on, the scene director uses a vertical shot list for a 9:16 frame on screen. It cuts between tight single-subject shots (whoever is climbing, fumbling or taking over is the only thing in frame) and a stacked duet: the two sides one above the other, split top and bottom, for face-offs and the verdict. The scoreboard and round dots sit in the side bars, and the frame carries a small Larpmaxxing watermark so clips advertise the game. Spectators and the other player are unaffected.
  • Funny muted: every fumble is readable with no sound, with captions for the key sounds.

The Ultimate LARPer: the game's mascot

An original character, and the face of the game on the icon, thumbnails, code announcements and loading screen. The silhouette should be readable from across the map: every stat at once, turned up to Maxxed. Oversized shades, a cardigan worn like a cape, a golden matcha in one hand, a golden dumbbell in the other, a tote bag of Monopoly money, and a very serious book under one arm.

Arrival (a 10-second sequence seen by the whole server):

  1. T−10s The sky dims, every screen in the Plaza glitches to his silhouette, and a server-wide warning reads "THE ULTIMATE LARPER IS ARRIVING".
  2. T−5s His theme music sting plays, and the ground in the Plaza starts to rumble.
  3. T−0s He arrives with one of several entrances, rotated each time: a private jet landing, a supercar convoy, a matcha geyser erupting from the fountain, or a helicopter lowering him on a golden rope ladder.
  • The fight: anyone can larp-off him once per appearance. His stats are always 1.15× the challenger's own, so it's always an underdog fight. With the upset roll that's a ~21% win chance at any rank.
  • Boss variants: his scenes follow the same tier rules, but every tier has a gold-plated version. Even his Tier 1 bus is gold.
  • The reward: a win pays 10× a normal larp-off bonus, a Legendary item, and a badge.
  • Getting exposed: when someone beats him, the whole server sees "omar_larps EXPOSED THE ULTIMATE LARPER" and he leaves with his own fumble exit.

He fills the same role as the Clavicular event in the mog games without using a real person, and because he scales to each challenger, a brand-new player has the same shot as a LARP Maxxer.

Touch Grass (rebirth)

Unlocks at LARP Maxxer. You "log off and touch grass": stats reset to 0 and rank resets to NPC. You keep your Wins, cosmetics, badges and every prestige reward.

The principle: a player who has touched grass 17 times should be impressive because of what everyone can see on them, not because their economy is broken. Farming bonuses shrink with each rebirth and stop at 2×. Prestige keeps growing.

At the 2× cap a rebirth takes roughly half the first run's time, about 3 hours, so reaching ×17 takes around 60 hours of play without passes or boosts. That's why it impresses people. The rebirth multiplier stacks with the 2× Pickups pass and boosts. All numbers are balancing targets.

HUD & UI

Design phone-first: most Roblox players are on mobile, so every element has to fit a small landscape screen with thumbs on it.

  • Top-left: rank title and a progress bar to the next rank, e.g. 4,210 / 6,000.
  • Left edge: five compact stat bars in their stat colours, with values.
  • Top-right: Wins, the event timer, and the Codes and Settings buttons.
  • During a larp-off: the HUD hides. Only the five round dots and the stat name stay, top-centre, and the verdict card fills the centre. See Built for vertical.
  • Challenge popup: challenger name, rank and Touch Grass count, Accept and Decline, a 10-second ring timer.
  • Settings: Accept larp-offs, Clip Mode, Show cosmetics, Reduce effects, Music and SFX volume.

Tech & data

Server-authoritative

The client only plays scenes and draws UI. Every number is decided on the server.

  • Pickups: validated on the server. The player must be within 12 studs of the item, and pickups are rate-limited per second.
  • Randomness: all rolls, including upsets, viral moments, spawns and fumble picks, happen on the server only.
  • Scene sync: at the start of each round the server sends one package to every client near the stage: both rolled values, both tiers, whether a Viral Moment hit and at which tier, and the fumble variant. Every viewer then plays exactly the same skit.
  • Remotes: every remote checks its arguments and is rate-limited.

The trend template

Everything specific to the trend lives in config modules: stats, items, ranks, scenes, events, rebirth rewards, tuning and text. When the next trend hits, you swap the config, the art, and the scene props and animations. Pickups, larp-offs, saving and leaderboards stay as they are.

Content rules

Moderation takedowns are what kill trend games, so these rules are fixed:

  • No real people. Every NPC is fictional, including the Ultimate LARPer, who is an original mascot. No streamer names or lookalikes.
  • No trademarks in-game. Use "blind-box plush" rather than Labubu, "supercar" rather than Lamborghini, "fake watch" rather than Rolex, "tumbler" rather than Stanley. Parody is fine as long as no real brand name appears.
  • Props, not looks. Nothing rates faces or bodies. That's the difference from the mog games, and it's safer with moderators and with parents.
  • Cartoonish fumbles. Slapstick is fine. Injuries, blood and humiliation that targets appearance are not.
  • Gender-neutral ranks, items and titles throughout.
  • Audio from Roblox's own licensed library only.
  • Maturity questionnaire: fill it in honestly. Content like this should come out at the lowest rating.
